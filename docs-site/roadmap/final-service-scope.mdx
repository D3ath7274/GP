**Adaptive IPS framework** is the canonical project name. Its final goal is a behavioral intrusion-prevention framework with a multi-vendor firewall-policy management layer. It will collect evidence, profile devices and traffic, analyze effective firewall policy, prioritize risk, propose changes and perform qualified, reversible containment or policy actions. SOC operators can inspect and reverse actions. APF identifiers in existing files, environment variables, APIs and schemas remain compatible.

This page records the target product scope. It does not announce delivery of the enterprise services. Every capability in the supplied specification's Section 85 is included below with a stable S01–S70 ID. The [delivery phases](/roadmap/delivery-phases) define dependencies, requirements and exit gates. Adding a goal to this page does not implement its parser, algorithm, workflow or adapter.

## Requirements beyond the numbered list

Section 37's custom risk profiles, Section 40's audit reporting, and Sections 75–76's evidence/finding contracts are required parts of P10/P11, alongside the 70 rows. Reports must include rule/object usage and cleanup, changes, compliance results, risk, behavioral associations, evidence versions, assumptions and unresolved coverage. Support machine-readable JSON/CSV and a human-readable report first; later PDF generation must preserve the same findings and lineage.

Observed transport flows cannot establish application transactions, authorization outcomes or business ownership without appropriate supporting sources. Section 77's module layout is a proposed decomposition; creating empty folders is not an acceptance gate.

Sections 78–83 guide sequencing, deterministic audit logic and measurement. Sections 84–87 describe the final product and summarize earlier requirements. They do not add an instruction to claim capabilities before their release gates pass. A specific vendor order is contingent on available lab equipment and faithful semantic coverage.

## Interpretation rules for implementation

- Maintain separate observation, detector verdict, independent ground-truth review, policy-risk, recommendation-confidence and enforcement-state fields. UNKNOWN is a valid result. A reviewed descriptive profile is not proof that every activity was benign.
- Keep duplicate/coverage/routing/NAT/reachability calculations deterministic for supported semantics. ML can classify behavior and prioritize evidence. Generated explanations cannot turn an unsupported policy feature into a proven result.
- RF and AE scoring shared rows are correlated. A signature can also share the same packet evidence. Record dependencies and validate combinations; do not multiply confidences or infer certainty from a vote count.
- Absence of observed use does not establish safe deletion. Require visibility, counter continuity, business/seasonal exceptions, owner context and before/after verification.
- Rule changes must preserve security-profile, schedule, identity, logging, inherited policy and stateful semantics, not only address/port overlap. Mark dynamic or unsupported application/user matches indeterminate.
- A missing log is a visibility finding, not proof of an attack. A broad rule is exposure context, not proof that permitted traffic is malicious. Country/ASN novelty, high entropy and periodicity require corroborating context.
- Compliance reporting covers supported technical checks and evidence. It does not certify an organization's compliance with an entire standard.
- Publish a tested compatibility matrix for vendor, firmware, management system, virtual context, read/write operations and verification. The extensible core is the universal goal; untested combinations stay unsupported or read-only.

## Final product acceptance

Each S ID needs an implementation reference, versioned input/schema, fixtures, negative/unsupported cases, resource/privacy limits, UI/API/report behavior and an operator procedure before status changes. Preserve independent tests for sensor/feature correctness, parser/normalization fidelity, deterministic audit/path correctness, campaign detection, calibration, recommendation safety and effective enforcement/recovery.

The final demonstration should link a behavioral event to a valid identity and contemporaneous policy revision, explain the effective permitting rule and path, produce a risk-aware recommendation, execute an authorized plan on a qualified adapter, verify containment and legitimate service continuity, and demonstrate expiry or SOC reversal. Model/profile changes must pass shadow and rollback gates. This scoped evidence cannot establish that no future attack will ever be missed.
