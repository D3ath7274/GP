Research review: **2026-11-05**. This is an APF engineering synthesis of primary standards, official analyzer documentation and research papers, not a ranking established by experiments on your home network. “Preferred” means the method worth testing under the stated assumptions. No method makes a finite feature set universally distinguish attacks from benign behavior.

## What has been implemented in this milestone

The [offline group-evidence tool](/deployment/feature-evidence) now fits frozen per-device/VLAN references, separately calibrates group maxima, reports the contributing measurements and retains insufficient-evidence outcomes. It does not change packet extraction, feature values/order, shipped RF/AE artifacts, online adaptation or firewall writes. Site inter-arrival mean/SD/CV are now cataloged under G1 and site size SD under G6, correcting their scientific grouping without changing either CSV schema.

The JSON companion reference/feature-methods.json covers every **102 legacy plus 71 site** column occurrence, with group, units, recommended computation, current limitation and source IDs. Recommendations requiring new state/parsers remain future work. G18–G24 stay planned.

## The method-selection rule

1. Define event time, direction, identity, key, byte layer, horizon and eligible denominator before selecting a formula.
2. Preserve exact counters and stable online moments; count missing/truncated/capped observations explicitly. Random timestamp subsampling does not preserve adjacent inter-arrival structure. [Stable moment algorithms](https://www.sandia.gov/research/publications/details/formulas-for-robust-parallel-computation-of-arbitrary-order-arbitrary-varia-2015-02-01/), [measurement selection](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc5475/).
3. Keep sample count and support. CV is SD divided by the mean and becomes unstable near a zero mean; its value alone does not identify an attack. [NIST CV](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman2/auxillar/coefvari.htm).
4. Compare like-for-like device/role/service/time contexts. A legitimate camera can be periodic and a backup can be asymmetric. Binding a score to an IP/VLAN still needs independent identity validation.
5. Evaluate robust residuals and empirical reference tails. A zero MAD is an explicit zero-scale condition, not a reason to manufacture a zero deviation. [NIST outlier diagnostics](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda35h.htm).
6. Calibrate the maximum within a group on separate reviewed windows, and separately calibrate the overall maximum. This avoids adding several correlated columns as though they were independent votes. It remains an APF candidate scorer requiring experiments.

## From five measurements to one group finding

For each feature j, the fit interval freezes its median m_j and the sorted absolute deviations d_ij. A later value uses s_j = -log2((1 + number of fit deviations at least abs(x_j - m_j))/(n_fit + 1)). The group statistic is max(s_j), not a sum of votes. The later calibration interval records group statistics; its inclusive upper-tail rank is (1 + number of calibration statistics at least the current statistic)/(n_cal + 1). A second maximum across the qualified groups is calibrated directly on complete calibration rows for the overall finding. Missing required measurements produce insufficient evidence.

The +1 rank/tie convention follows [conformal outlier research](https://doi.org/10.1214/22-AOS2244). Formal validity needs assumptions about reference/test sampling; network time series have dependence and drift, so this implementation makes no distribution-free real-network false-alarm guarantee. See [non-exchangeable calibration](https://jmlr.org/papers/v25/23-1553.html). A tail rank is not malicious probability, and repeated windows/devices still need a campaign-level alert/action budget.

For example, G1's current site mean, SD and CV may all change during one workload shift. That is one rhythm finding with supporting values. With low mean/SD support, a clock-resolution problem or a legitimate newly periodic sensor, the correct result can be insufficient evidence or an unusual-but-benign finding. Confirm attack mechanisms using G11/service outcomes, signatures or independent application evidence before claiming malicious behavior.

## Per-group and per-feature recommendations

Each table row names an actual current column. Formulas below are recommended measurement contracts, not a claim that every legacy proxy already implements the recommendation. Compare the stated current limits with source and the [existing rationale](/architecture/feature-rationale). ## G1: Timing and burst rhythm

**Preferred method:** Use adjacent event times, stable moments and explicit support. Compare per peer/service and multiple horizons; low CV also describes ordinary IoT telemetry.

**Current boundary:** Legacy timestamp subsampling above 2,000 observations changes adjacent gaps; burst durations are milliseconds. Site only exports mean/SD/CV, not extrema or episodes.

Primary basis: [Pébay, numerically stable one-pass moments](https://www.sandia.gov/research/publications/details/formulas-for-robust-parallel-computation-of-arbitrary-order-arbitrary-varia-2015-02-01/), [NIST coefficient of variation](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman2/auxillar/coefvari.htm), [RFC 5475 packet selection and measurement interpretation](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc5475/), [Kitsune, NDSS 2018](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/ndss2018_03A-3_Mirsky_paper.pdf).

| Schema / column | Units | Recommended measurement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| legacy / `inter_arrival_mean` | seconds | Mean of consecutive event-time gaps in a declared direction/key; retain interval count and timestamp resolution. |
| legacy / `inter_arrival_std` | seconds | Sample SD of consecutive event-time gaps using stable online moments; at least two valid intervals. |
| legacy / `inter_arrival_cv` | dimensionless | Sample SD / positive mean of gaps; report interval count, ties/reorders and timestamp resolution. |
| legacy / `inter_arrival_min` | seconds | Minimum genuine adjacent event-time gap; distinguish quantized timestamps and duplicate observations. |
| legacy / `inter_arrival_max` | seconds | Maximum adjacent gap, with idle/window censoring recorded rather than invented connection termination. |
| legacy / `burst_count` | episodes | Count disjoint over-threshold subwindow episodes using a threshold frozen from reference data; qualify timescale. |
| legacy / `burst_duration_avg` | seconds, new contract | Mean duration of defined episodes; legacy emits milliseconds and needs an explicit versioned conversion. |
| site_v3 / `inter_arrival_mean` | seconds | Mean of consecutive event-time gaps in a declared direction/key; retain interval count and timestamp resolution. |
| site_v3 / `inter_arrival_std` | seconds | Sample SD of consecutive event-time gaps using stable online moments; at least two valid intervals. |
| site_v3 / `inter_arrival_cv` | dimensionless | Sample SD / positive mean of gaps; report interval count, ties/reorders and timestamp resolution. |

## G2: Directionality and asymmetry

**Preferred method:** Use canonical biflow identity, declared originator/responder direction and matching exposure/byte layer. Preserve no-reply and unseen-reply as distinct states.

**Current boundary:** Legacy reverse aggregates are IP-pair/protocol, not full sessions; +1 ratio smoothing and raw reply ratio are not success probabilities.

Primary basis: [RFC 5103 bidirectional flow export](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5103.html), [Zeek connection log](https://docs.zeek.org/en/master/reference/logs/conn.html).

| Schema / column | Units | Recommended measurement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| legacy / `fwd_packet_count` | packets | Count originator packets in an identity/context-bound canonical bidirectional session or declared device window. |
| legacy / `bwd_packet_count` | packets | Count matched responder packets for that same canonical session/context; report observation coverage. |
| legacy / `fwd_bwd_packet_ratio` | dimensionless | Forward / reverse packet count with explicit denominator and undefined/no-reply state; smoothing must be declared. |
| legacy / `fwd_bwd_bytes_ratio` | dimensionless | Forward / reverse bytes at the same layer; preserve zero-denominator state and optional declared log-ratio. |
| legacy / `fwd_avg_packet_size` | captured bytes | Forward captured bytes / forward packets; null with no forward support. |
| legacy / `bwd_avg_packet_size` | captured bytes | Reverse captured bytes / reverse packets; null with no reverse support. |
| legacy / `reply_rate` | fraction or named packet ratio | Use matched request-response successes / eligible requests for true responsiveness; raw reverse/forward counts are a different metric. |

## G3: TCP lifecycle proxies

**Preferred method:** Use bounded persistent sequence-aware TCP state, deduplicated attempts, explicit timeout/censoring and matched completion outcomes.

**Current boundary:** Legacy completed_sessions is a 0/1 window proxy and incomplete_ratio uses +1 smoothing. Exact session state is planned.

Primary basis: [RFC 9293 TCP state and sequence semantics](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9293.html), [Zeek connection log](https://docs.zeek.org/en/master/reference/logs/conn.html).

| Schema / column | Units | Recommended measurement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| legacy / `syn_ack_ratio` | dimensionless | Separate initial SYN attempts from retransmissions; compare eligible handshakes with matching SYN-ACK/ACK progression. |
| legacy / `completed_sessions` | sessions | Count sequence-consistent established sessions in a bounded persistent five-tuple state machine, not a window-level Boolean. |
| legacy / `incomplete_ratio` | fraction | Uncompleted eligible attempts / eligible attempts after an explicit timeout; distinguish pending, censored and unobserved. |
| legacy / `avg_session_duration` | seconds | Mean actual completed-session lifetime by declared start/end criteria; unfinished sessions are censored. |

## G4: Distribution entropy

**Preferred method:** Use categorical frequencies, Shannon H=-sum(p*log2(p)), count, richness and top share together. Test bias/normalization and distribution divergence under matched support.

**Current boundary:** Legacy size entropy is captured-frame size, not application payload. Entropy needs sample/context controls; high or low alone is not malicious.

Primary basis: [Lakhina/Crovella/Diot feature-distribution research](https://cs-pub.bu.edu/faculty/crovella/anomalies.html), [RFC 5475 packet selection and measurement interpretation](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc5475/).

| Schema / column | Units | Recommended measurement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| legacy / `payload_size_entropy` | bits | Shannon entropy of the declared length categories; captured-frame size and payload length must have different names/contracts. |
| legacy / `src_port_entropy` | bits | Shannon entropy of source-port frequencies at a source/context/horizon scope; report sample count and category richness. |
| legacy / `icmp_type_entropy` | bits | Shannon entropy of observed ICMP family/type/code categories; separate IPv4/IPv6 and diagnostic traffic. |

## G5: ARP binding behavior

**Preferred method:** Use chronological ARP request/reply and time-valid authenticated lease/port/binding context. Retain gratuitous/probe/failover controls.

**Current boundary:** Legacy unsolicited/binding heuristics are not exact request matching. ARP does not cover IPv6 NDP and first-seen bindings can be poisoned.

Primary basis: [RFC 5227 legitimate IPv4 probes and announcements](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/rfc5227/), [RFC 8520 intended device communication](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8520.html).

| Schema / column | Units | Recommended measurement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| legacy / `arp_reply_rate` | messages/second | Observed ARP replies / actual elapsed observation interval; retain count and packet loss context. |
| legacy / `arp_request_rate` | messages/second | Observed ARP requests / actual elapsed interval; classify legitimate probes/announcements separately. |
| legacy / `arp_reply_request_ratio` | dimensionless | Replies / requests with explicit zero state; exact request/reply correlation is preferable for unsolicited claims. |
| legacy / `arp_gratuitous_count` | messages | Count defined ARP announcement forms without equating legitimate announcements with poisoning. |
| legacy / `arp_unsolicited_count` | messages | Time-correlate replies with outstanding requests and verified binding evidence; record expired/missing request visibility. |
| legacy / `mac_ip_binding_changes` | binding events | Count changes against time-valid MAC-to-IP sets/leases, allowing legitimate multi-address ownership. |
| legacy / `ip_mac_binding_changes` | binding events | Count conflicting IP-to-MAC claims against verified leases/ports/time; separate DHCP reuse/failover. |

## G6: Packet-size distribution

**Preferred method:** Use stable sample moments plus declared size-band proportions. Retain support, frame layer/MTU/offloads and matched-volume benign controls.

**Current boundary:** Legacy small-packet boundary is below 64 captured bytes; site currently exports the outgoing size SD and BASE mean only.

Primary basis: [Pébay, numerically stable one-pass moments](https://www.sandia.gov/research/publications/details/formulas-for-robust-parallel-computation-of-arbitrary-order-arbitrary-varia-2015-02-01/), [RFC 5475 packet selection and measurement interpretation](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc5475/).

| Schema / column | Units | Recommended measurement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| legacy / `pkt_size_std` | captured bytes | Sample SD of explicitly defined captured sizes using online moments; keep n and compare protocol/role context. |
| legacy / `pkt_size_variance` | captured bytes squared | Sample variance M2/(n-1); ablate against SD because the two encode the same scalar information. |
| legacy / `small_pkt_ratio` | fraction | Count sizes below a declared byte boundary / observed packets; boundary and encapsulation are part of the schema. |
| legacy / `large_pkt_ratio` | fraction | Count sizes above a declared boundary / observed packets; qualify MTU/offload effects and sample support. |
| site_v3 / `packet_size_std` | captured bytes | Online sample SD of outgoing sizes, null below two observations. |

## G8: Broadcast and multicast context

**Preferred method:** Use separate Ethernet broadcast/multicast and ARP denominator counts with VLAN/site context. Preserve legitimate discovery and malicious storms.

**Current boundary:** Current site does not reproduce the complete legacy network-ratio set; high broadcast share is not an automatic benign exemption.

Primary basis: [RFC 5227 legitimate IPv4 probes and announcements](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/rfc5227/), [RFC 5475 packet selection and measurement interpretation](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc5475/).

| legacy / `broadcast_ratio` | fraction | Broadcast frames / all frames at the identical observation boundary and horizon. |
| legacy / `multicast_ratio` | fraction | Multicast frames / all observed frames; distinguish multicast from Ethernet broadcast in the implementation. |
| legacy / `arp_broadcast_ratio` | fraction | Broadcast ARP messages / observed ARP messages; retain support and legitimate address-resolution controls. |

## G11: Reconnaissance evidence

**Preferred method:** Combine exploration across hosts/services, sparse flag context and matched response outcomes. Measure per source/target/subnet with several horizons.

**Current boundary:** Existing counters/sets are device-window summaries and response proxies; exact probe matching, deduplication and session support remain work.

## Select models by measured yield

The installed comparator uses marginal feature deviations and their maxima. It does not learn joint covariance, causal attack chains, service-specific state or time-of-day workload modes. A changed combination of otherwise familiar marginal values can be missed; a harmless new workload can be unusual. Discrete/constant references also limit rank resolution. These are reasons to compare it against qualified joint models and mechanism-specific evidence, not reasons to call every group flag malicious.

Start with the transparent group-rank baseline and the existing RF as separate comparators. Evaluate group-specific autoencoders or lightweight ensembles as candidates; [Kitsune](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/ndss2018_03A-3_Mirsky_paper.pdf) supports studying efficient multi-scale channels and autoencoder ensembles, not assuming the same accuracy transfers here. Per-group models can also miss cross-group interactions, so evaluate a combined model and mechanism-level corroboration.

Fit preprocessing and any resampling only inside a training split defined by campaign/device/time before rows are generated or mixed. Reserve calibration and later benign/attack tests. Calibrate RF scores on independent data and check reliability as well as discrimination; [scikit-learn calibration](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration) explains why training predictions bias a calibrator. Keep AE reconstruction deviation separate from probability.

Optimize recall at a specified false-alert/action budget, with per-family recall, false alerts per device-day, missed/abstained coverage, precision-recall behavior, detection delay and CPU/drop/memory/storage measurements. Use group removal/retraining, matched-volume benign controls, changed tools/rates/payloads, new devices and later-time/site holdouts. [Security ML pitfalls](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity22/presentation/arp) and [operational NIDS limits](https://gangw.cs.illinois.edu/class/cs562/papers/Closed_World-IDS-sp10.pdf) motivate these controls. Accuracy on detector-generated labels is not independent confirmation.

## Implementation order

1. Use the existing golden/restart checks and real-data pilot to qualify the measurement boundary.
2. Use this offline reference scorer to inspect supported current measurements. Review all excluded groups, missing support and benign alerts before interpreting yield.
3. Implement exact session/probe correlation and reported sample support first (G2/G3/G11/G14); add packet-contact long-horizon state and qualified change statistics (G13). Compare EWMA and CUSUM under benign workload transitions, rather than assuming a universal threshold.
4. Add bounded protocol reassembly and explicit encryption/unsupported outcomes (G15–G17), then independently bound resource/identity/control-plane/application outcomes (G18–G24).
5. Train/calibrate schema-bound models and evaluate independent campaigns. Wire only qualified evidence into the later durable action lifecycle and vendor adapters, with separate containment/rollback proof.

This advances the P2/P3 research foundations of S01–S07/S10–S11. It does not complete those services, provide online model promotion, prove unknown-attack recall or qualify automatic firewall reconfiguration.
